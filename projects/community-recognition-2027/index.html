Ideas that help us see
what is possible.

Four areas of contribution, explored through the questions, frameworks and collaborations behind the work.

How does a promising technology become a viable business?

Concepts that clarify

Making the logic of a business visible.

With Alexander Osterwalder and Yves Pigneur, Christopher Tucci helped clarify the business model concept: its vocabulary, uses and relationship to information systems. Their 2005 paper gave a more precise language to a term used in many different ways.

With Lorenzo Massa and Allan Afuah, his 2017 review distinguished three perspectives: what a firm actually does, how people understand its business, and how that business is formally represented. Separating these perspectives makes it easier to see what an innovation changes.

Why it matters

A clearer language for designing, comparing and questioning how businesses work.

Osterwalder, A., Pigneur, Y., & Tucci, C. L. (2005). Clarifying business models: Origins, present, and future of the concept. Communications of the Association for Information Systems, 16, 1–25.

Read the paper

Massa, L., Tucci, C. L., & Afuah, A. (2017). A critical assessment of business model research. Academy of Management Annals, 11(1), 73–104.

Read the paper

When can a crowd solve a problem better than familiar experts?

Searching beyond the familiar

The right answer may come from elsewhere.

With Allan Afuah, Christopher Tucci explained how crowdsourcing can transform a difficult search for distant expertise. A problem unfamiliar to an organisation may be routine for someone outside it; broadcasting the challenge lets those people recognise it and choose to respond.

Their framework also identifies the conditions that matter: a problem that can be clearly communicated, capable and motivated solvers, and a solution that can be assessed and put to use. It helps explain when opening a search is likely to work.

Why it matters

Design the problem and the invitation so that relevant expertise can find you.

Afuah, A., & Tucci, C. L. (2012). Crowdsourcing as a solution to distant search. Academy of Management Review, 37(3), 355–375.

Read the paper

What happens to a technology that does not fit the existing business?

From unused ideas to new ventures

Giving good ideas a path into the world.

With Henry Chesbrough, Christopher Tucci connected open innovation with Lean Startup. Their 2020 paper explains how experiments can help discover and validate business models for technologies that an established company has left underused.

It also examines why those experiments can be difficult inside a large firm: new ventures encounter established roles and ways of working. Bringing external knowledge in, or finding routes to market outside the firm, can help companies organise the search for new value.

Why it matters

Innovation requires a route to market, a business model to test and room to learn.

Chesbrough, H., & Tucci, C. L. (2020). The interplay between open innovation and lean startup, or, why large companies are not large versions of startups. Strategic Management Review, 1(2), 277–303.

Read the paper

Which decisions turn AI’s potential into value?

From technical promise to organisational choices

Understanding what it takes to create value with AI.

With Gianvito Lanzolla and Simone Santoni, Christopher Tucci developed a framework for the strategic and organisational trade-offs involved in AI adoption. Their 2021 study of financial services compared that framework with media and institutional narratives from 2013–2019, finding a gap between the choices involved and the stories being told.

He also helped connect AI research with education as Co-Director (Education) of Imperial’s I-X initiative, bringing perspectives from business, engineering, science and medicine into shared programmes.

Why it matters

Put organisational choices and learning at the centre of the AI conversation.

Lanzolla, G., Santoni, S., & Tucci, C. L. (2021). Unlocking value from AI in financial services: Strategic and organizational tradeoffs vs. media narratives. In M. Pagani & R. Champion (Eds.), Artificial intelligence for sustainable value creation (pp. 70–97). Edward Elgar Publishing.
